About TIIER

Bridging borders through research, education, and advocacy

The Institute of Immigrant Education and Research is a new regional hub at Rowan University's College of Education, dedicated to transforming the landscape of immigrant education.

Who We Are

TIIER supports collaborative research across local, national, and global institutions, publishes and shares research on immigrant education, and offers educational programming and learning opportunities for researchers, educators, and communities.

We work at the intersection of academic rigor and community empowerment, engaging education and policy stakeholders, advocating for immigrant communities, and building their capacity to enrich U.S. society.

Collaboration

Building a bridge between local schools, organizations, and global research institutions to serve education and research needs.

Dissemination

Making sure immigrant education research doesn't stay in academic journals, but reaches the people who can use it for policy and practice.

Innovation

A space where policy stakeholders and educators co-design solutions for the future of education and the well-being of immigrant generations.

Strategic Goals

Research & Dissemination

  • Fostering collaborative research in immigrant education across local and global institutions
  • Digesting and disseminating critical research insights for policy and practice

Educational Programming

  • Creating workshops, training academies, and professional development for P-12 and non-profits
  • Offering unique learning opportunities for immigrant students and families

Advocacy & Engagement

  • Engaging policy stakeholders to catalyze systemic change and innovation
  • Building capacity in immigrant communities to enrich U.S. society

Collaboration Ecosystem

  • Serving as a regional hub for local, national, and global research collaboration
  • Developing robust partnerships with higher education and companies

Leadership

Dr. Zeynep Isik-Ercan

Co-Director
Professor of Early Childhood Education

Zeynep Ercan is a Professor of Early Childhood Education, co-director of Early Childhood Leadership Institute, a professional development and research institute and co-director of the Institute of Immigrant Education and Research at Rowan University, New Jersey. She leads large scale research, training, and evaluation projects, writes for academic journals, and teaches and speaks on best practices for culturally and linguistically diverse children, teachers, families and communities, intellectual development, and professional development of educators and leaders. Zeynep’s research has appeared in academic journals such as Young Children, Race, Ethnicity and Education, Childhood Education, International Journal of Science Education, Early Childhood Education Journal, and Diaspora, Indigenous and Minority Education Journal.

Zeynep is actively involved in local, national and global scale educational and social issues by consulting with international educational agencies, serving as the President of National Association for Early Childhood Teacher Educators, a founding board member of Society of North American Scholars, cofounder and cochair of Institute of Academic Excellence, a national academic mentoring program for first generation scholars, and as a board member, volunteer and donor for multiple non-profit and community organizations. She is the recipient of 2014 The Indiana Early Care and Education Award, 2017 Wall of Fame Award for Teaching/Advising at Rowan University, 2020 Excellence in Engagement Award at Rowan University, 2022 Outstanding Early Childhood Teacher Educator Award from New Jersey Association of Early Childhood Teacher Educators (NJAECTE) and 2024 Teamwork University award at Rowan University (as the Woman Alliance Network Board).

ercan@rowan.edu

Dr. Seung Eun (Sunny) McDevitt

Co-Director
Associate Professor of Early Childhood Education

Seung Eun “Sunny” McDevitt is an Associate Professor of Early Childhood Education in the College of Education at Rowan University. Born in South Korea, she immigrated to the U.S. during her adolescent years. Her research focuses on understanding and supporting diverse teachers of color, particularly immigrant women of color and other minoritized teachers, and their inclusive pedagogies in early childhood and teacher education settings. She earned her doctorate in Curriculum and Teaching from Columbia University with a focus on early childhood education and teacher education. Dr. McDevitt’s work is grounded in the principles of equity and inclusion and is significantly informed by the many years of working with young children, families, and teachers from marginalized immigrant communities in New York City as a teacher, early interventionist, and professional developer. She is a recipient of several grants from prestigious institutions such as the American Educational Research Association, the Mother Cabrini Health Foundation, and the European Educational Research Association. Additionally, she has published extensively in a wide range of journals, including Teachers College Record, Teaching and Teacher Education, and the Journal of Research in Childhood Education.
